# Evidence for the Wallex six-strategy experiment

Retrieved **2026-10-21**. This is a research record supporting [the implementation plan](implementation-plan.md), not a performance report. Published results below are authors' or organizers' claims; none was reproduced by this research task. Proposed experiment changes are our inferences. Actual local results must come from saved run artifacts.

The earlier Trade-Bot research notes were discovery leads only. The links below were checked again against primary papers, author repositories, official documentation and organizer disclosures. Indexed copies are explicitly distinguished from pages fetched today. No new package, model or dataset was downloaded by this task; no account or trading operation was performed.

## What changes today's experiment

1. Keep the six methods on identical closed Wallex bars, decision times, capital, exposure limits, test timestamps and costs. Rank against cash and buy-and-hold, then inspect turnover and drawdown. Architecture size alone is not evidence of an advantage.
2. Treat public spot backtests as **research**, not challenge-account certification. The supplied challenge requires the organizer's credit-account market permissions. Package restrictions, account fees and competition rules remain separate checks.
3. Keep historical LLM and pretrained forecasting results flagged for possible training-data contamination. A date-free numerical packet reduces obvious lookup cues but cannot prove the model never saw a price sequence. Record model/checkpoint identity, prompts, raw outputs, failures, timing and token/cost receipts.
4. Use the installed sklearn/PyTorch stack and existing local Kronos assets. Installing four trading frameworks today adds integration work without resolving Wallex account permissions or historical execution evidence.
5. Costs must matter to selection. At a hypothetical 0.35% fee each side, one full round trip needs roughly 0.70% gross price movement before spread/slippage; this is arithmetic, not a verified challenge fee. Report sensitivity rather than claiming this fee is known.

These are experimental recommendations, not expected returns. Supporting evidence follows.

### Observed public API behavior in this workspace

The parallel acquisition task obtained real unauthenticated HTTP 200 responses on 2026-10-21 from `/v1/markets`, `/hector/web/v1/markets`, `/v1/udf/config`, candle history, depth and trades. Its acquisition manifest (`../data/acquisition_manifest.json`; local supporting artifact) binds URLs, receipt times, raw body paths and SHA-256 hashes. These are local observations, distinct from the indexed documentation above.

One important deviation was independently checked against the saved raw response during this research: the request specifying `resolution=15` returned **1,440 bars with 60-second spacing**, not fifteen-minute bars. The probe receipt (`../data/raw/1792589457369569600_probe_15.receipt.json`; local supporting artifact) identifies the request and body (`../data/raw/1792589457369569600_probe_15.body`; local supporting artifact). Recomputed body SHA-256 was `b818945768cc4fa40cc774ed4eac353c55166b937dc25a8b2f7e76054c562ba3`, matching the receipt. A resolution=5 probe returned a payload error despite HTTP 200. The data owner also observed misleading `no_data` for multi-day ranges and therefore paginated one-minute history by day. HTTP success and a requested interval alone cannot certify a candle dataset.

The lab's fifteen-minute CSVs are locally aggregated from observed one-minute bars, retaining only complete buckets. They are **derived candles**, not native fifteen-minute responses. During review, negative missing-interval counts exposed a pandas timestamp-unit reporting defect. The data owner confirmed the cause and repaired it with an explicit nanosecond conversion and gap assertions. Read the refreshed manifest/quality files for final counts; the initial reporting defect did not change CSV timestamps or aggregation counts.

## Research studies: useful mechanisms and limits

| Study and evidence level | Verified finding | Adaptation worth testing today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [CryptoTrade, EMNLP 2024; arXiv v2 revised 2026-08-17](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09546v2), author historical experiments | Combines on-chain/off-chain analysts, a trading decision and reflection. The limitations section explicitly acknowledges underperformance against traditional signals, despite broader abstract claims. Daily decisions and limited data constrain transfer. | A bounded numerical LLM allocation challenger is a fair ablation. Without point-in-time news/on-chain data it is an adaptation, not a CryptoTrade reproduction. Preserve losing/invalid decisions too. |
| [FinAgent, KDD 2024, inspected v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18485v1), author historical experiments | Multimodal intelligence, memory, reflection and tools. The paper reports lower ETH returns than FinMem and attributes degradation to stock-specific auxiliary strategies; adding those rules harms ETH in its ablation. | Do not transplant XAU signals, trading sessions or stock tools. Test fusion against its best individual expert; additional roles can degrade transfer. |
| [MacroHFT, KDD 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14537), author historical experiments | Proposes memory/context-aware RL to address policy overfitting and changing market conditions. Its action/environment design is a separate research question from generic forecasting. | Regime-dependent expert weighting is a hypothesis for fusion. Do not call a supervised tree or neural predictor an RL reproduction, or train RL without validating its simulator/reward. |
| [Profit Mirage, 2025-10-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07920), author leakage study | Explicitly studies LLM information leakage and reports deterioration outside model knowledge windows; proposes leakage-robust evaluation and counterfactual perturbations. This is not a Wallex replication. | Historical LLM returns remain contaminated until convincingly shown otherwise. Freeze prompts and evaluate future unseen bars; keep mechanical causality checks separate from backbone pretraining uncertainty. |
| [Can AI Make Money in Crypto?, 2026-09-28 v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510v1), author staged benchmark | Separates historical, prospective exchange paper and real-money evaluation. Reports none of four selected live methods beating BTC buy-and-hold during 2026-08-24–2026-09-21. The text inconsistently says five live methods in the protocol and four in results; a later sentence dates selection to Aug 23. Short live duration and selection prevent universal family rankings. | Adopt the staged protocol and measure latency/execution discrepancies. Do not adopt its winning backtest architecture as an established live winner. Our common long-only task is stricter than comparing methods with different action spaces. |
| [DXRG production-record artifacts](https://github.com/ProjectDXAI/continuous-record-llm-trading-agents), operator research disclosure | Public repository distinguishes real-ETH and mixed paper/small-real-capital fleets; says its measured pre-alpha fleet showed no aggregate directional edge. Aggregate figures and replay are not complete independently reconciled private trajectories. The repository explicitly separates the historical fleet from the current product. | Record every decision and a fixed mechanical exit baseline. Do not tune a bracket using this project's test period or copy their measured bracket as if it were universal. |

None of these citations independently proves that an LLM, RL policy, neural network or ensemble will improve Wallex net returns. Our six-variant run tests that question locally under disclosed assumptions.

## What remains unresolved

- Contest-account allowlist, account selection mechanism, fees/credit economics, executable precision/minimums and MCP schema are not certified by this report.
- Wallex documentation search indexes contain stale pages. Current endpoint behavior must be substantiated by saved response bytes and receipt timestamps from acquisition.
- Candle simulation cannot establish fill probability, queue priority, market impact, outages or account/order recovery. A current book snapshot only describes its receipt time.
- Historical pretrained-model results cannot remove training overlap merely by splitting local data chronologically.
- Six methods and any parameter searches create selection bias. Report all attempted runs/failures; selection happens on validation only. Any test-driven repair/selection makes that test a development set and requires a later untouched prospective interval.
- A three-day contest may be too short to distinguish skill from exposure and luck. Report net marked account value using the organizer's rules, plus turnover, drawdown, costs and execution failures; do not infer dependable future returns from one window.
